# 16 — Learned dispatching policy (reinforcement learning)

Status: R1 (features). Training (R2), planner (R3) and evaluation (R4) follow; their sections are marked as such.

## 1. Prose description

A fourth planner next to CP-SAT ([12](12_cp-sat.md)), the GA ([14](14_genetic-algorithm.md)) and MCTS
([15](15_mcts.md)):
- It builds the plan with the dispatcher ([11](11_dispatching-rules.md)), from the snapshot with its frozen part, as
  MCTS does.
- At each decision it picks a candidate with a learned policy instead of searching, so a plan costs one pass.
- The decisions are the same as in MCTS: the iterations with more than one eligible candidate, overtime variants
  included (hook `choose`, [11](11_dispatching-rules.md) §5).

Building a JSP or FJSP plan step by step is a Markov decision process (Lv et al., 2025, p. 1, p. 3). Lv et al.
distinguish three kinds of training on benchmarks (p. 2, p. 13): instance by instance, per instance class, and
size-agnostic. The policy here is meant to be size-agnostic: it scores every candidate with the same network and
then normalizes the scores over the candidates of the decision. That way it works for any number of operations,
machines or candidates.

## 2. Sets, parameters, variables

New symbols (central definitions in [notation.md](notation.md)):

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\mathcal{C}_\zeta$ | eligible candidates of decision $\zeta$: $(t, o, m, p)$, or $(t', o, m, p, \overline{\mathrm{ot}})$ for an overtime variant |
| $\bar p$ | mean shortest processing time of the instance, $\frac{1}{\lvert O \rvert}\sum_o \underline{p}_o$ (at least 1), the time unit of the features |
| $\mathrm{ft}(c)$ | feature vector of candidate $c \in \mathcal{C}_\zeta$, 18 values |

**Features** (`FEATURE_VERSION = 1`). $t^0 = \min_{c \in \mathcal{C}_\zeta} t_c$ is the time of the decision.
$\mathrm{wr}$, $\ell$, $V_m$, $V_z$ are the dispatcher's state ([11](11_dispatching-rules.md)).
`log1p` means $\ln(1 + x)$; `symlog` means $\operatorname{sign}(x) \ln(1 + \lvert x \rvert)$.

| Name | Value |
|---|---|
| p | $p / \bar p$ |
| wait | $\ln(1 + (t - t^0)/\bar p)$ |
| p_rel | $p$ over the mean $p$ of $\mathcal{C}_\zeta$ |
| work_left | $\ln(1 + \max_{k \in k(o)} \mathrm{wr}_k / \bar p)$ |
| remaining_path | $\ln(1 + \ell_o / \bar p)$ |
| path_rel | $\ell_o$ over the largest $\ell$ in $\mathcal{C}_\zeta$ |
| due_slack | $\operatorname{symlog}((d_o - t - \ell_o)/\bar p)$ with $d_o$ the earliest due date of $k(o)$; 0 without one |
| has_due | 1 if $o$ has a due date |
| machine_load | $V_m$ over the mean machine load; 0 without machine |
| worker_load | least $V_z$ over the workers qualified for $o$'s shares, over the mean worker load; 0 without workers |
| has_workers | 1 if $o$ has shares |
| overtime | 1 for an overtime variant |
| batch_step, s_batch_step, run_head | 1 if $o$'s step batches (p-batch), is an s-batch step, or $o$ heads a run |
| successors | $\ln(1 + \lvert \mathrm{succ}(o) \rvert)$ |
| work_done | $1 - \sum_k \mathrm{wr}_k / \sum_k \mathrm{wr}^0_k$, the same in every row |
| candidates | $\ln(1 + \lvert \mathcal{C}_\zeta \rvert)$, the same in every row |

Why these:
- Every value is scale-free: times in units of $\bar p$, loads relative to their mean.
- Some values are relative to the other candidates of the decision. The network scores each row on its own, so
  these give it the context of the decision.
- The values cover what the rules use: $p$ (SPT), ready time (FIFO: the wait), $\mathrm{wr}$ (MWKR), $\ell$ (LRP),
  due dates (EDD).
- They also cover what no rule here sees: worker loads, overtime, batching.

## 3. Policy and training

**Policy.** One network scores every candidate from its features alone:
$\mathrm{sc}(c) = \mathrm{MLP}(\mathrm{ft}(c))$, with two hidden layers of 64 ReLU units and one output. Over the
candidates of a decision,
$$\Pr(c \mid \mathcal{C}_\zeta) = \frac{e^{\mathrm{sc}(c)}}{\sum_{c' \in \mathcal{C}_\zeta} e^{\mathrm{sc}(c')}}$$
The softmax normalizes over however many candidates there are. A plan is built greedily (the highest score) or, in
training, sampled. Load and s-batch partners are ranked by SPT. Overtime variants are offered when the instance has
workers and a calendar.

**Training (REINFORCE with a shared baseline).** One gradient step per iteration, $N^{\mathrm{upd}}$ steps in all:
1. Draw a training instance from the generator: the base file `dental-carriers-workers.json`, seed $10000 +$
   iteration, a random number of orders in $[10, 40]$.
2. Sample $N^{\mathrm{smp}}$ plans $\pi_j$ with values $f_j$. The baseline is their mean $\bar f$ (shared
   baseline, see the sources below), so no rule plan is needed per instance. The advantage is divided by the
   standard deviation $\mathrm{sd}_f$ of the $f_j$ (0 if all plans are equal):
   $$\mathrm{adv}_j = \frac{\bar f - f_j}{\mathrm{sd}_f}$$
   This is scale-free and weighs every instance the same; a plan better than the mean has a positive advantage.
   - First runs divided by $\bar f$ instead and learned nothing over the untrained network (§5c).
   - On the generated instances the makespan varies by only a few per mille between samples. So $(\bar f - f_j)/\bar f$
     made instances with tiny spreads count as little as their spread.
3. Loss: $-\frac{1}{N^{\mathrm{smp}}}\sum_j \mathrm{adv}_j \sum_\zeta \ln \Pr(c_{j,\zeta} \mid \mathcal{C}_{j,\zeta})$
   over the decisions of plan $j$. Adam, learning rate $3 \cdot 10^{-3}$.
   - **Lexicographic objective** (R2b): the advantage is taken on the first level on which the $N^{\mathrm{smp}}$
     plans differ (own). The one weighted number $f$ ([04](04_workers.md) (O3)) would also give the levels' order,
     but after the division by $\mathrm{sd}_f$ a sample that differs on the top level would make every lower level
     vanish. With one level per objective, the first level that differs decides exactly the order $f$ gives.
4. **Validation** before the first step and every 25 steps, on 20 instances (seeds 9000–9019, 25 orders, never
   trained on). The mean of the greedy plan's value divided by:
   - `validation_ratio`: the best of the 15 rule plans;
   - `validation_ratio_ot`: the best heuristic. That is the 15 rule plans and the 5 joint rules that take overtime
     whenever the operation they pick can start earlier with it (`overtime_rule_plans`).

   The second yardstick is the fair one: the rules never plan overtime, the policy can (§5c). Below 1 means better
   than every heuristic.
5. **Checkpoint:** the returned weights are those with the lowest `validation_ratio_ot` over all validations, the
   untrained network (before the first step) included. So a run never returns a policy worse on validation than its
   start.

Defaults: $N^{\mathrm{upd}} = 300$, $N^{\mathrm{smp}} = 8$. One thread with fixed seeds (torch and the instance
draw), so a run repeats on one machine. The dental examples (seed 1) and the Fidentis case are not trained on.

## 4. Objective function

The planner's objective, as for the GA and MCTS. Training takes it as $f$: makespan, or the worker objective with
cutoff slack ([04](04_workers.md) (O7)). Under makespan overtime costs nothing, so a policy trained on makespan may
learn to plan overtime (as the GA does, [15](15_mcts.md) §5a).

## 5. Implementation in code

- `simulation.planners.rl_features`: `Featurizer(ci)` computes the static values once ($\bar p$, total work, due
  date per operation, run heads). `featurizer(eligible, state) -> np.ndarray` gives one `float32` row per
  candidate. `NAMES` and `FEATURE_VERSION` are defined there.
- `DispatchState.worker_load` ($V_z$) is the dispatcher's own list of booked share minutes per worker, so nothing is
  counted twice. `Checkpoint.restore` keeps the state and the dispatcher on one copy.
- Tests (`tests/test_rl_features.py`):
  - one finite row per candidate on the toy instances and dental workers;
  - the overtime flag marks exactly the variants;
  - worker loads only grow, and a resumed run sees the loads of the uninterrupted run at every decision;
  - hand case `late_pack`.
- `notebooks/dispatch_speed.py`: all 1710 plans unchanged.
- `simulation.planners.rl` (R2):
  - `Policy` (the MLP);
  - `rl_plan(ci, policy, snapshot, generator, log_probs)`: greedy, or sampled with the log-probabilities for
    training;
  - `save_policy` / `load_policy`: `.npz` + `.json`. The JSON holds the architecture, feature names and version,
    the training arguments, the commit and the log. Loading refuses another feature version.
- `simulation.rl_train` (R2): `train(...)` and the command line
  `uv run python -m simulation.rl_train --out models/<name> [--objective worker] [--iterations] [--samples]`. It
  writes `<name>.npz`, `<name>.json` and `<name>.csv`. It needs nothing but the repository and
  `uv sync --extra rl`, so it also runs on Kaggle.
- Tests (`tests/test_rl.py`):
  - the greedy plan is feasible, repeats, and the DES realizes it unchanged (zero disturbance), on the toy
    instances and dental workers;
  - a sampled plan records one log-probability per decision, and gradients reach every layer;
  - saving and loading without pickle gives the same scores;
  - another feature version is refused;
  - training repeats for a seed and moves the weights;
  - the command line writes all three files;
  - the training and validation seeds miss the evaluation instances;
  - **training learns:** on toy-dag-3x4 alone (every rule plan 12, the non-delay optimum 11, [15](15_mcts.md) §5), 150
    steps with 16 samples take the greedy policy to 11. In a check it did so from three different initializations,
    after 100–125 steps.
- **Dependency:** PyTorch 2.15 (CPU build, index "pytorch-cpu" in `pyproject.toml`), since 2026-11-27 the optional
  extra `rl` (`uv sync --extra rl`); without it `tests/test_rl.py` is skipped. Weights are stored as `.npz` with JSON
  metadata, never with `torch.save`, which pickles.

## 5c. First training runs (2026-11-23)

Setup: $N^{\mathrm{upd}} = 300$, $N^{\mathrm{smp}} = 8$ (2400 plans), instances with 10–40 orders, one core, about
9 min per run. Validation: 3 instances with 25 orders. Ratio = greedy value / best rule value, mean over the 3; below
1 means better than every rule. OT = planned overtime in minutes on the 3 instances. Models: `models/rl-makespan`,
`models/rl-worker` (`.npz`, `.json`, `.csv`).

| Objective | Untrained network | Trained, advantage $/\bar f$ (first run) | Trained, advantage $/\mathrm{sd}_f$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| makespan | 0.985 (OT 434, 185, 386) | 0.986 (OT 180, 64, 156) | 0.981 (OT 207, 222, 183) |
| worker objective | 0.722 | 0.736 | 0.876; best on the way 0.573 after 100 steps |

**Findings:**
- **The untrained network already beats every rule** (0.985 and 0.722). It comes from the overtime candidates: the
  rules never plan overtime, the network does at random. So the ratio against the rules is no measure of learning;
  the untrained network is the baseline to beat.
- **Training learns on a single instance** (§5, toy-dag-3x4 from 12 to the optimum 11), so the gradient pipeline
  works.
- **On the instance distribution, 300 steps learn nothing stable.**
  - Makespan: 0.981 against 0.985, within noise. The makespan of a generated instance varies by only a few per mille
    over the choices, so there is little to learn.
  - Worker objective: unstable. Best after 100 steps (0.573), 0.876 at the end, worse than the untrained network.
  - The policy learned to plan less overtime (the second level of the objective), but not stably less tardiness.
- **The budget is small.** 2400 plans against 5000 training episodes in Zhou et al. (2026, p. 14, Zotero
  `EA8QC55F`) and 10,000 training instances in Li et al. (2026, p. 11, Zotero `6GGUPZNL`). The validation (3 instances)
  is noisy.

**Open (R2b):**
- Keep the best checkpoint by validation.
- More validation instances.
- A longer run (Kaggle).
- Compare against the untrained network and against a rule that takes overtime whenever it can, not only against
  the plain rules.
- For the worker objective, possibly a reward per level instead of the one lexicographic number.

## 5d. State when paused (2026-11-23)

**R2b is implemented and tested, but not yet trained with:** lexicographic advantage, 20 validation instances, the
overtime-rule yardstick, the best checkpoint. A 1000-step run was stopped unfinished when the user paused the RL
work. The models in `models/` are still those of §5c.

**Next steps, as discussed with the user:**
1. **Imitation pretraining (R2c).**
   - Generate many small instances with varied setups: machine counts, workers, due-date tightness, durations, via
     `model_copy(update=...)` of the generator parameters.
   - Solve them with CP-SAT where it reaches optimality (about 5–15 orders, makespan first), otherwise with the GA
     as teacher.
   - Turn each plan into one label per decision: the eligible candidate whose operation starts first in the
     teacher's plan, on the teacher's machine. Replaying a teacher's plan through the non-delay dispatcher can drift
     away from it; log that "teacher gap" per instance.
   - Store the labels as JSON Lines; train with cross-entropy.
   - The cost (about 1000 × 30 s of CP-SAT) suits Kaggle or the cluster.
   - Source for imitation learning in scheduling: **[uncertain]**, none found in the library.
2. **RL fine-tuning** of the pretrained policy with the existing `rl_train`.
3. **Architecture:** self-attention across the candidates of a decision (a Transformer encoder layer before the
   score), so candidates see each other. Lv et al. (2025) list MLP, CNN, RNN, attention/Transformer and GNN (p. 1)
   and pointer networks (p. 2). A plain MLP over the whole state needs a fixed size (p. 5). GNNs over the
   disjunctive graph are common for FJSP (p. 7, p. 8), but they need features of every operation.
4. **R3:** `rl_planner` with the planner interface and `PolicyConfig "rl"`. **R4:** evaluation against rule, GA,
   MCTS and CP-SAT on the dental examples and Fidentis.

## 6. Assumptions and open points

- The features are own choices, not taken from a source. Whether they suffice shows only in training (R2).
- No order or step identity enters the features, so the policy can transfer across instances. The cost is that it
  cannot learn step-specific behaviour beyond the batch flags.
- The worker load feature takes the least loaded qualified worker. The dispatcher picks by least load too, but per
  share and with the share's interval free.
- Training instances come from the generator. The dental examples and Fidentis are kept for evaluation (R4).
